# Reference Composition Analysis

## What the references establish

### 1. Omotive — modular maximalism

- A near-black editorial canvas creates contrast for oversized, playful display type.
- Type is treated as an illustration: stacked, multi-color, cropped at the edges.
- Small rounded modules create proof points (metrics, brands, product tiles) without becoming a generic feature grid.
- Sticker motifs and rough marks establish a tactile, crafted layer.

**Apply to Puff:** use the black anchor surface sparingly for hero statements, high-value proof clusters, and experimental portfolio frames. Keep Puff yellow as the primary signal, then use one supporting accent for a controlled editorial cluster.

### 2. Check — typographic collage

- Condensed display words create a reading path before any paragraph is read.
- Cropped image rectangles act as glyph substitutes and interrupt a rigid baseline deliberately.
- Supporting copy is small, isolated, and positioned in the negative space around an oversized idea.
- Visual hierarchy is produced by scale discontinuity, not by adding components.

**Apply to Puff:** build one large proposition per key section. Pair it with 1–2 media/illustration inserts; do not use an equal-weight collection of cards.

### 3. Behance FAQ/contact — restraint plus gesture

- FAQ uses a narrow readable column, hairline rules, predictable row height, and one high-saturation utility control.
- The contact block introduces color as a large rounded panel instead of fragmenting it among many elements.
- A lime scribble crosses the panel but preserves label legibility.

**Apply to Puff:** FAQ should feel calm and precise, in deliberate contrast to the expressive hero. Let one Puff-yellow or lime gesture overlap a panel only where it does not cover actionable text.

### 4. Recruitment UI — utility module hierarchy

- High contrast is distributed through large rounded blocks, black gaps, and bright data surfaces.
- Control groups are pill-shaped and visibly stateful.
- Dense schedule information remains readable because typography, spacing, and surface layers are systematic.

**Apply to Puff:** pricing comparisons and process/dashboard previews can use utility-module framing. Dense information requires explicit labels, grid alignment, and a calm neutral surface beneath bright signals.

### 5. Grumpi — terminal brand gesture

- The footer is an experience, not an afterthought: navigation is compact above a gigantic brand wordmark.
- Warm yellow canvas and deep textured ink create a friendly but distinctive close.
- Newsletter is integrated as a wide pill rather than a utility form dropped beneath links.

**Apply to Puff:** end the page with a high-recognition brand block: compact navigation/CTA above an oversized Puff wordmark or logo. Avoid a conventional four-column corporate footer.

## Shared grammar

1. Oversized typography is structural, not ornamental.
2. Strict grids coexist with controlled, intentional violations.
3. Color appears in fields/panels rather than as a full-spectrum background treatment.
4. Rounded modules communicate utility; irregular marks communicate personality.
5. Negative space protects legibility after expressive moments.

## Anti-patterns

- Mimicking a reference's cobalt/lime/pink palette as Puff's primary palette.
- Forcing collage overlap onto readable FAQ, legal, or pricing detail.
- Building every section as a black maximalist canvas.
- Using decorative squiggles near form labels, pricing numbers, or accordion controls.
- Replacing content hierarchy with oversized type alone.
